## Scope and unseen-word audit

Reviewed every checkpoint, its English/French instruction and feedback, required basic/rule tokens, prior teaching displays and notes, current generated fields and both Anki verification reports.

All 14 targets are new lexical examples relative to the earlier course questions and teaching notes. A substring search finds あさ inside おばあさん and おかあさん, but those are different previously taught words; the word あさ meaning “morning” has not been taught. No other checkpoint target appeared in the earlier displays/notes inspected.

This makes them unseen within this course on first presentation. It does not guarantee that an individual learner has never encountered the words elsewhere. On later scheduled reviews they are familiar practice items, not fresh unseen-word assessments.

## Objective, cues and feedback

Executed English and French inputs() and checked all 14 generated questions. Each front contains only the Japanese target plus a generic reading-check label. Meta is empty; meaning is confined to the answer-side Example. Answer romaji and audio are withheld until reveal. There is no translation, cognate, grid-position or pronunciation hint before the attempt.

The learner compares the attempted reading with the revealed model. Feedback explicitly values accurate unaided decoding, without a speed threshold or vocabulary test. It identifies useful timing/feature checks: marked consonants, contracted sounds, vowel length, doubled consonants and the nasal's timing beat. English and French preserve the same criteria.

The checkpoints test reading of isolated words. They do not introduce grammar, particle-use readings, handwriting requirements or automatic speech assessment. Native claim/check and reveal-based feedback remain appropriate, but these self-checks are not objective certification of mastery.

## Prerequisites and current cumulative order

Each checkpoint requires all 92 basic forms plus its relevant derived-rule tokens. All prerequisites are presented earlier in the current order.

The combined-rule cases are supported: サッカー and がっこう follow both length and small-tsu teaching; しゃしん and きゅうり apply the previously taught contracted-sound principle; ファイル, チェーン and ディナー use introduced extensions. The word prompts add no previously untaught rule.

In the reviewed 286-question expansion, the checkpoints occupy positions 273–286 and mix hiragana and katakana. English and French use the same sequence. Existing core sound-recall reverses retain at least one intervening question after each prerequisite reading introduction. The overall stage order remains hiragana basics, hiragana rules/applications, katakana basics, katakana rules/applications, then cumulative reading.

Source prerequisites control authored exposure order, not mastery gates. No later card is conditioned on a successful grade. Finishing these 14 introductions does not mean reviews should stop.

The reviewed 286-item inventory has a mathematical minimum of 48 introduction days at six new questions/day. Reviews, pauses and later additions extend that period; this is not a fixed schedule or a measured ten-minute workload.

## Separate integration issue identified

The brief explicitly names grouped ji/zu answers when the relevant marked forms are covered. The current 286-item inventory teaches those forms but contains only the two grouped o listening questions.

The author has agreed to add four selected grouped listening applications: じ/ぢ and ず/づ, plus ジ/ヂ and ズ/ヅ. These fit the selected-derived-application scope without requiring an exhaustive derived-form matrix. They are to follow both relevant marked-family introductions with intervening material.

This finding concerns complete-course conformance, not the validity of the 14 checkpoint items. The supplement and resulting 290-question order require a separate final integration review before they are claimed as approved.
