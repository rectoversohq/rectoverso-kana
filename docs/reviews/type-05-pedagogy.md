## Scope and objective

Reviewed all 12 records, six English/French shape explanations, current emitted fields and introduction order, the six distinct light proof images, and both revision-2 Anki verification reports.

The pairs are ぬ/め, る/ろ, ね/れ, シ/ツ, ソ/ン and ク/ケ. Each member is the target of one question. The learner hears a sound and identifies its written form among the two visible candidates.

This is targeted sound-to-form discrimination/recognition, not unaided recall of a written form. A binary guess can be correct; success alone cannot demonstrate independent sound-to-kana recall or handwriting knowledge. The type is useful supplementary practice because the separately authored core listening questions already elicit uncued recall.

**Wording refinement verified:** All current prompts say “Listen. Identify the matching kana.” / “Écoutez. Identifiez le kana correspondant.” This accurately describes the visible-choice task. The native claim/check and reveal-based flow remains appropriate; no custom multiple-choice or automatic grading mechanism is required.

## Cues, feedback and prerequisites

The emitted front contains the two kana and the requested audio, with no romaji or target highlighting. The answer contains the target kana, romaji and matching audio. The feedback names a visible discriminating feature and explicitly asks the learner to attend to the kana rather than its screen position.

All target/audio/romaji mappings match the basic inventory. Both forms in every pair are introduced before the comparison. Neither position nor “left/right” is an accepted answer.

Inspected all six distinct light proof images. The stated loop/open-ending differences for the hiragana pairs and the relative stroke/sweep/bar differences for the katakana pairs match those pictured forms. The descriptions are shape cues, not compulsory handwriting or stroke-order instructions. English and French express equivalent practical distinctions.

The proof images are not native screenshots and are not the package's rendered native text; native typeface, spacing and visibility must still be checked.

## Order, bias and review load

Executed current source ordering and English/French inputs() expansions.

- Current combined inventory: 264 questions, including these 12.
- In each script, the three first targets are followed by the three second targets.
- Each pair's second target has exactly two other comparison questions between it and its first target; partner questions are not adjacent.
- Each pair's two answers occupy opposite sides. Across the 12 questions there are six left and six right targets, so a constant-side strategy does not succeed throughout.
- Display order remains fixed within each pair. Position memory can still assist recognition over repeated encounters; the instruction does not eliminate that possibility. Uncued listening and later reading transfer remain the stronger evidence of independent learning.
- Existing basic reading/listening reverses retain at least one intervening question.

Twelve targeted additions are a bounded review burden aligned with the brief. They consume the same six-new-question allowance and do not require an extra comparison for every character. The current inventory alone needs at least 44 introduction days at that cap, before pauses or future additions; this is not a fixed completion promise or evidence that reviews fit ten minutes.
